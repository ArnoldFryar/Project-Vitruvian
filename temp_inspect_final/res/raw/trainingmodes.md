# Non-Adaptive Modes
The load will not adjust as long as movement is performed within range-of-motion.

<section>

## Old School
Old School applies constant resistance on the way up and down.

<details>
<summary>How fast/slow should I move?</summary>

Because the load will not change, you can move at a normal speed&mdash;steady and controlled is recommended. There is no need to pause at the top or the bottom.
</details>

<details>
<summary>How can it benefit me?</summary>



* Strength development
* Build muscle
* Isometric hold
* Tempo
</details>

<details>
<summary>Advanced Options</summary>

**Progression**: pause at the top, +1, 2 or 3kg \
**Regression**: pause at the top, –1, 2 or 3kg
</details>

<details>
<summary>Spotter</summary>

If you struggle intensely without moving the weight it will eventually reduce the weight.  
If you move the weight down during the upward movement the weight will release for safety.
</details>
</section>

<section>

## Eccentric Only
The selected load is applied during the lowering phase of the exercise. Concentric is the fancy word for the movement of raising the weight and eccentric is the word for the movement of lowering the weight.  You can slowly lower more weight that you can raise.  
Eccentric Only is a great strength builder which can be safer for your joints than lifting heavy on the way up.

<details>
<summary>How fast/slow should I move?</summary>

When you reach the top of the movement, wait 2 seconds for the weight to load. *(Very heavy weights may take a little bit longer to load.)* When the weight is fully loaded slowly lower the weight for 5 full seconds.  Pause at the bottom for the weight to unload.  Then you will raise the weight with minimal resistance and pause again at the top.

**Speeds**: Up at a normal speed, pause at the top for 2 seconds, 5 seconds to go down.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How can it benefit me?</summary>

* Strength development
* Build muscle
* Overcome plateaus
</details>
</section>

# Adaptive Modes
The load adjusts as the machine intuitively calculates the appropriate weight for your strength level at any given time.

<mark>**Important**</mark>: These modes are designed to maintain tension, if you use momentum it will *feel jerky* as the machine thinks it needs to apply heavier load for you due to your faster pace.

You can command the maching to adjust load by:
* **Pausing at the top**: increase load
* **Pausing at the bottom**: decrease load

<section>

## Time Under Tension
Designed to push your limits. Pause at the top of your rep to load on force, control the force on the way down, then pause at the bottom to deload just enough force to lift the cables back up. Try setting the weight just above what you can normally lift and use the loading/deloading to find the perfect force for the concentric and eccentric phase of each rep.

<details>
<summary>How fast/slow should I move?</summary>

Move up relatively quickly, pause at the top for 2 seconds to reload the weight if the Trainer gave you a spot on the way up. Take 5 seconds to slowly lower the weight.  Do not pause at the bottom.

Up 1-2 seconds, pause at the top for 2 seconds, 5 seconds to go down.
</details>

<details>
<summary>How can it benefit me?</summary>

* Focus on controlled technique without worrying about load
* Train through fatigue
</details>

<details>
<summary>Advanced Options</summary>

**Progression**: pause at the top, +1, 2 or 3kg \
**Regression**: pause at the top, –1, 2 or 3kg \
**Beast**: fast increase in load & longer time spent to deload (as per above command)
</details>

<details>
<summary>Spotter</summary>

Time Under Tension will spot you on the way up and reload the weight for the way down if you pause at the top. If you pause at the bottom the machine will think you can’t lift the weight and the weight will be rapidly reduced.
</details>
</section>

## Pump
Lift fast, then control the weight on the way down (eccentric phase). Lifting faster will get you closer to your selected force and locks it in for the eccentric phase. Lifting slower will reduce the force.

<details>
<summary>How fast/slow should I move?</summary>

Fast&mdash;think speed.
</details>

<details>
<summary>How can it benefit me?</summary>

* Metabolic conditioning
* Low rep power/explosive training
* HIIT
</details>

<details>
<summary>Advanced Options</summary>

**Progression**: pause at the top, +1, 2 or 3kg \
**Regression**: pause at the top, –1, 2 or 3kg
</details>

<details>
<summary>Spotter</summary>

Machine will auto-deload for your safety: when you move slow
</details>
</section>

<section>

## Echo
Echo Mode uses isokinetic training principles to keep the speed steady while recording your effort at every position.

It learns your strength curve and ensures the eccentric loading matches your concentric effort, creating a force map that echoes your best performance.
<details>

<summary>How to get the most from echo?</summary>

Push as hard as you can from the bottom to the top of your lift.
Maintain intensity throughout the full range of motion.
</details>

<details>
<summary>How can it benefit me?</summary>

* Strength Testing: When you would like to test how strong you really are.
* Injury Management: Maintain and improve strength without risking overexertion.
* Technique Focus: The consistent speed lets you refine your movements.
* Less Friction: No need to guess your weight—just start lifting.
* Safety: Safer and more efficient for building strength and muscle growth.
* Efficiency: Get a time-effective workout tailored to your abilities.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Spotter</summary>

Machine will auto-deload for your safety: when you go **below** your calibrated bottom range-of-motion or if you **push gently** on the way up.
</details>
</section>

# Trainer+ Training Program Cheat Sheet

## Traditional Resistance Training
|             | Example Traditional Program                | Suitable Training Mode                                                                                  |
|-------------|--------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Main Lift   | Back Squat 4&nbsp;×&nbsp;5 @&nbsp;80%      | Old School                                                                                              |
| Accessories | Lat Pulldown 3&nbsp;×&nbsp;12 @&nbsp;60%   | Old School <br>+ Time Under Tension <br>_(for the set when fatigue kicks in and technique breaks down)_ |
| Isolation   | Hamstring Curls 3&nbsp;×&nbsp;12           | Eccentric Only<br>Time Under Tension                                                                    |

## Advanced Training
|                        | Example Format                     | Suitable Training Mode |
|------------------------|------------------------------------|------------------------|
| Tempo                  | Deadlift 3&nbsp;×&nbsp;8 (3:1:1:1) | Old School             |
| Metabolic Conditioning | DB Chest Press 3&nbsp;×&nbsp;10    | Pump                   |
| To-Fatigue Set         |                                    | Time Under Tension     |

## Other Training Styles
|                   | Intention                                                                                     | Suitable Training Mode |
|-------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------|
| Power             | Moving a solid amount of force in a short amount of time                                      | Pump                   |
| Skill Development | When learning technique with a slow & controlled tempo.<br>e.g. Squats, Deadlift, Bench Press | Time Under Tension     |
